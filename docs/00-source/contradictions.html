C1 — Depth of the rules engine — the central conflict — needs your input

Position A (S1 §2.2, §6.2): "Prioritize playability, speed, and deck testing usefulness over complete MTG rules accuracy. The application should avoid implementing a full MTG rules engine until later versions require it." S1 explicitly excludes the priority system, stack resolution, trigger handling, and replacement effects from scope.

Position B (S2 §3, §8): official MTG priority rules, official state-based actions checked to fixpoint, official replacement/prevention ordering, the full continuous-effects layer system with dependency and timestamp rules, mana as explicit pool objects, official combat, and official copy rules — all marked MVP, and all marked "Completed" in the spec's own status tracking.

Resolution: Position B. S2 is newer and much more specific, replacing two sentences in S1 with roughly 40 defined behaviors.

Impact: Position B makes the MVP roughly 10× larger. M2 still delivers a manual sandbox before rules work begins; see milestones.

Decision needed: rejecting Position B shrinks or removes M3 and M4 and makes Simple Bot Mode the plan.

C8 — Priority timeout vs. manual advancement — worth a look

S2 §3 specifies both a 5-second priority timeout and manual turn advancement by default.

Resolution: keep manual advancement authoritative and ship auto-pass as an opt-in setting. Its default is [PROPOSED] P3.

C13 — Fixed single-screen battlefield vs. iPhone-first — worth a look

S2 requires a fixed, non-scrolling battlefield for an iPhone-first Commander app. An iPhone holds roughly 12–15 fixed-size cards, while Commander boards often reach 20–40.

Resolution: ship the simpler fixed battlefield. If space fails in use, add pinch-zoom or “Clean Up Board”; both remain [DEFERRED] in features.

Decision needed: confirm that shipping the simpler layout before its mitigation is acceptable.
