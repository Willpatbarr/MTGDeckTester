Apple Developer Program

Enrollment and signing for M7 distribution. The bundle ID is permanent.

What & why

The Apple Developer Program is what lets the app run on a device for longer than 7 days and be distributed via TestFlight to anyone besides the developer. Without it, a free Apple ID still builds and runs the app on a physically connected device, but the build expires weekly.

There is no real alternative under consideration — this is iOS distribution's single gatekeeper. The only "alternative" is staying on a free Apple ID and re-signing every 7 days, which is fine solo but breaks the moment anyone else needs the app.

Adoption trigger

Adoption trigger

T1 — Before first real user Precisely: the first time you want the app on a device you can't re-plug into your Mac every week. In practice, this is the first tester. A free Apple ID is sufficient for solo development up to that point.

Setup steps

  1. Have (or create) an Apple ID under the account that will own this app long-term — this becomes the Team.
  2. Enroll at developer.apple.com as an Individual (not Organization — see the teammate-delta warning below before deciding). Pay the $99/yr fee.
  3. In Xcode, Signing & Capabilities, set the Team to the newly enrolled account.
  4. Choose the bundle identifier: com.<yourname>.mtgdecktester.
  5. Register the app's App ID in the developer portal (usually automatic the first time Xcode archives with that bundle ID).
  6. Create the app record in App Store Connect, matching the same bundle ID.
  7. Set up a TestFlight internal testing group and add the first tester's Apple ID email.
  8. Verification: archive a build in Xcode, upload to App Store Connect, wait for TestFlight processing (5–30 minutes), and confirm the tester receives and can install it via the TestFlight app.
Painful to change later

The Team ID and bundle identifier are effectively permanent. The bundle ID (com.<yourname>.mtgdecktester) is tied to the App Store record for the app's entire lifetime — changing it later means starting over as a new app with no shared history, reviews, or TestFlight testers. Pick it once, carefully, before the first archive.

Escape hatch

Lock-in: High. There is no alternative distribution path for iOS — this is the one gatekeeper. Once TestFlight testers and an App Store Connect record exist, they cannot be moved to another provider; the entire relationship is Apple-specific.

What lives only here: the App Store Connect app record, TestFlight tester list and build history, and any App Store reviews if the app is ever published publicly. There is no meaningful "migrating off" — this is why M7 is treated as a real milestone rather than an afterthought.

Teammate delta

Sharpest edge in the whole stack

An Individual Apple Developer account cannot add team members at all — there is no seat model, no invite flow, nothing. A second person who needs to build, sign, or release this app needs either their own separate Apple Developer account, or the whole project needs to re-enroll under an Organization account, which requires a D-U-N-S number and typically takes weeks to obtain and verify. This is the single highest-friction teammate blocker in the entire service stack — plan for it well before a second contributor is imminent, not after.

What to do next

  1. Do not enroll yet — wait for the T1 trigger (first tester who isn't you) as tracked in M7.
  2. When the trigger fires, decide Individual vs. Organization before enrolling, based on whether a second contributor is realistically imminent — Organization's D-U-N-S lead time makes switching later expensive.
  3. Continue to the service map or on to milestones.
