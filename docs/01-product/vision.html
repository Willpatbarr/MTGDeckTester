Vision

What this is: why MTG Deck Tester exists and what "done" is supposed to feel like.  What to do with it: read this before any product or scope argument, and settle disagreements against it.

One-sentence version

MTG Deck Tester is a solo-built, offline-first SwiftUI iOS app that lets one person play full, rules-accurate Commander games against AI opponents to test their own decks — no accounts, no backend, no commercial ambitions today.

Problem

Without this tool, testing a Commander deck means physically shuffling a paper deck, or squinting at a spreadsheet, or trusting gut feel about whether a build actually works. None of that tells you how a deck plays across repeated hands, against real pressure, or against the specific cards it's built to interact with.

The app exists to answer six recurring questions a deckbuilder actually has:

  • Is this deck consistent — does it do its thing often enough across many games?
  • What do opening hands and mulligans actually look like with this build?
  • Is the mana curve and colour base actually workable in practice, not just on paper?
  • How does the deck fare in specific matchups against other strategies?
  • Does a new card I'm considering adding actually improve the deck?
  • Can I practise a specific line of play — a combo, a sequencing puzzle — reliably and repeatably?

Users

Today there is exactly one user: the solo developer building the app. Friends may get access later via TestFlight (see M7), which is a possibility worth building toward, not a committed plan.

Be honest about this

"Customers" is a maybe that lives in the Someday tier of the feature list, not a roadmap item. There is no monetization, no accounts, and no server — see features.html's explicitly-out-of-scope list. Nothing in this project should be built as if a commercial user is coming.

What success looks like

Success is defined milestone by milestone, in the language of definitions-of-done from the milestone plan. Two are the clearest markers:

MilestoneWhat "success" means here
M2 — Game State & Manual Play SandboxA complete Commander game, playable entirely by hand from setup to a natural finish, with nothing enforced and nothing blocked. This is the first moment the app is genuinely usable for deck testing, even before any rules are automated.
M1 — Foundations, Card Data & DecksA pasted 100-card decklist resolves against Scryfall within 30 seconds, with images cached and available offline.
M3 — Rules CoreThe engine independently enforces priority, stack, triggers, SBAs, layers, and mana — deck testing stops depending on the user remembering the rules.
M4 — Combat & Copy EffectsCombat and copy effects resolve by official rules without manual adjudication.
M5 — AI OpponentA bot plays a full turn against you through the real decision pipeline — matchup testing becomes possible without a second human.
M6 — History, Persistence & ReplayUndo, redo, save, load, and deterministic replay all work — a bad line can be rewound and retried.
M7 — Ship to a TesterA friend installs the app via TestFlight, imports their own deck, plays a game against a bot, and a crash they hit reaches the developer with a usable stack trace. This is the first time the app exists for someone other than its author.

Product shape

  • Platform: iOS, iPhone-first UI, universal architecture, built in SwiftUI.
  • Format: Commander only for now — 40 life, commander rules on, 100-card singleton assumed even though legality isn't checked yet.
  • Data: offline-first. Card data ships from a Scryfall bulk download and is cached locally; the app works with no network connection after first launch.
  • Accounts: none. No sign-in, no server-side state, no sync.
  • Backend: none. This is a local, on-device app — see the services rejection list in 03-services.

The defining product principle

Strict engine, permissive UI

The engine is rules-accurate: it follows official MTG priority, state-based actions, replacement effects, and the full continuous-effects layer system. The UI is a testing tool, not a rules referee — it shows you every hand, every library, every zone destination, and blocks nothing, legal or illegal.

Visibility and legality are still tracked in the model for a future rules-aware mode, but MVP deliberately favours testing clarity over hidden-information secrecy. Do not "fix" this later by mistake — it is a decision, not an oversight.

Remember only 3 things

It's a testing sandbox, not a game client. The point is to answer "does my deck work," not to hide information from a solo player or gate them behind rules enforcement in the UI.
The engine is real, the audience isn't yet. A full, rules-accurate MTG engine is being built for an audience of one, with friends as a maybe. Scope the engine seriously; scope the audience honestly.
M2 before rules. A fully playable-by-hand sandbox ships before any rules subsystem is written, so the project is never more than one milestone away from being useful.
